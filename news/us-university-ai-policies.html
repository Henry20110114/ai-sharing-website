美国高校如何规定 AI 的使用：从芝加哥大学的禁令说起How US universities regulate AI use, starting with the University of Chicago ban

芝加哥大学的社科核心课从 2026 年秋季起禁用电子设备和 AI。本文整理了新规的内容和读者的反应，并对比了另外五所美国高校的做法。From fall 2026, the University of Chicago's social sciences core bans devices and AI in class. This piece summarizes the rules and readers' reactions, then compares five other US universities.

芝加哥大学：社科核心课禁用电子设备和 AI

来源：The Chicago Maroon: SOSC Core to Institute AI Ban, Technology-Free Classrooms This Fall

相关规定总结：

  1. 课堂层面：社科核心课变为无电子设备课堂。学生在课堂上禁止使用电脑、手机，禁止录音录像；学生主要阅读纸质资料参与线下讨论。仅有两种例外：残障学生经允许可以使用电子设备；教师出于特定教学目的可临时放开设备。
  2. AI 使用限制：作业禁止使用 AI 辅助写作；AI 批改作业也被禁止。但并非完全锁死 AI：教师在和学部领导报备之后，可以开展少量 AI 课堂教学实验，做试点探索。
  3. 政策初衷：学部主任 Jenny Trinitapoli 在备忘录写明，社科核心课首要目标是教会学生不依赖 AI 完成阅读、写作、独立思辨，如同训练学生跳出固有主流观念进行思考；课堂去掉电子设备，也是为减少分心，保障面对面深度讨论，让学生可以自主思辨、试错、交换观点。

读者的反应

文章下方读者众说纷纭：一部分观点批评禁令治标不治本——只能管控课堂时间，学生课后依然可以私下使用 AI，监管很难落地；还有读者提出，真正的 AI 教育应当改造教学环境而不是退回纸笔模式。另一部分支持者认同，基础本科课程应当优先保护人的独立思考，AI 不能替代课堂真人对话。

其他五所高校怎么做

同时我也搜索了哈佛大学、斯坦福大学、麻省理工、密歇根大学等主流顶尖高校的最新 AI 学术规则，各校均无极端的全校 AI 禁令，整体以包容、引导、规范为主。

哈佛大学

无统一 AI 禁用条例，将 AI 使用权限下放至各科任课教师，由老师在课程大纲中明确本课程的 AI 使用规则。核心要求为“透明披露”，学生使用 AI 辅助学习、写作、修改内容时，必须主动标注 AI 使用痕迹，隐瞒 AI 使用行为将被认定为学术不端。同时严格禁止学生利用 AI 撰写升学申请文书，违规者会被取消录取资格。

斯坦福大学

态度最为开放，2026 年 6 月全新更新校园 AI 体系，向师生开放官方授权的各类主流 AI 工具。学校明确鼓励学生合理利用 AI 辅助学习，部分商科、工科课程甚至要求学生使用 AI 完成作业实践。仅课堂闭卷考试场景禁止使用 AI，课外作业、课题研究均可合规使用，核心原则是“善用工具、拒绝代写”，重点培养学生驾驭 AI 的能力。

麻省理工学院

2026 年 8 月发布最新 AI 教学指导框架，不禁止 AI，而是对 AI 使用场景做精细化分类。将学习场景分为禁止使用、可使用需披露、无需披露、强制使用 AI 四大类。同时通过改革考试形式、增加实操项目等方式规避 AI 作弊问题，尤其在计算机、工程类专业，将 AI 作为必备学习工具融入日常教学。

密歇根大学

采用全美最通用的规范化管理模式，不禁止 AI 使用。要求学生所有核心作业必须填写标准化 AI 使用声明，清晰标注 AI 是否参与构思、起草、修改等环节。全程以“透明化”为核心，不限制工具使用，只约束学术诚信，杜绝学生隐瞒 AI 使用行为。

普渡大学

2026 年 4 月更新正式 AI 政策，明确鼓励学生负责任地使用生成式 AI。学校统一配备合规校园 AI 工具，禁止学生使用私人未授权 AI 处理校内科研、隐私数据。课程层面依旧遵循任课教师规则，所有 AI 辅助内容必须主动标注。

我的看法

总体来说除了芝加哥和斯坦福两个学校比较有特色以外，其他学校都采取较为中立的态度：允许使用但是需要公开自己在什么地方用了 AI。我很认可这个方式，一方面教师可以通过学生的 AI 痕迹检查学生是否真正通过完成这项任务提升了自己的思维能力，很清楚学生有哪些部分是靠自己做出来的，从而更好指导学生如何正确使用学习工具。对于学生来说不会有太多限制，只要愿意标注痕迹，可以完全按照自己的想法做一项任务，只要保持诚实可以很大程度上减少一些规则上的限制。同时，麻省理工把作业的 AI 使用要求分为 4 个部分的规则我也非常认同，这些给了学生们一个很清晰的规则：如何正确使用 AI 才能保证自己的能力得到全方位的锻炼。而普渡大学鼓励学生使用 AI 并提供 AI 的新规也非常合理，为了保障数据安全给学生提供授权的 AI 是一种很稳妥的做法。

芝加哥和斯坦福的核心态度比较鲜明，一个属于全面鼓励学生使用，一个是全面禁止，个人预测这两个学校的规则会慢慢改变，越来越像其他几所学校的政策靠拢，因为不论是过度鼓励还是过于严格禁止都会带来一些问题。

The University of Chicago: no devices and no AI in the social sciences core

Source: The Chicago Maroon: SOSC Core to Institute AI Ban, Technology-Free Classrooms This Fall

Summary of the rules:

  1. In the classroom: the social sciences core becomes a device-free classroom. Students may not use laptops or phones in class, and recording audio or video is prohibited; they mainly read printed materials and take part in in-person discussion. There are only two exceptions: students with disabilities may use devices with permission, and instructors may temporarily allow devices for specific teaching purposes.
  2. Limits on AI: AI-assisted writing is banned for assignments, and grading with AI is banned too. AI is not completely locked out, though: after reporting to the division's leadership, instructors may run a small number of classroom experiments with AI as pilots.
  3. The reasoning behind the policy: in her memo, the division's head, Jenny Trinitapoli, wrote that the primary goal of the social sciences core is to teach students to read, write and think independently without relying on AI, much like training students to think beyond prevailing mainstream views. Removing devices from the classroom also reduces distraction and protects deep face-to-face discussion, so that students can reason for themselves, make mistakes and exchange views.

Readers' reactions

Readers in the comments were divided. Some criticized the ban as treating the symptom rather than the cause: it only controls class time, students can still use AI privately afterwards, and enforcement is hard to carry out. Others argued that real AI education should reshape the learning environment rather than retreat to pen and paper. Supporters, on the other hand, agreed that foundational undergraduate courses should give priority to protecting independent human thinking, and that AI cannot replace real conversation in the classroom.

What five other universities do

I also looked up the latest academic AI rules at leading universities such as Harvard, Stanford, MIT and the University of Michigan. None of them has an extreme campus-wide AI ban; overall they favor inclusion, guidance and regulation.

Harvard University

There is no unified rule banning AI. Decisions on AI use are delegated to individual instructors, who set out the AI rules for their course in the syllabus. The core requirement is "transparent disclosure": when students use AI to help them learn, write or revise, they must label where AI was used, and hiding AI use is treated as academic misconduct. Using AI to write college application essays is strictly prohibited, and violators lose their offer of admission.

Stanford University

Stanford takes the most open stance. In June 2026 it rolled out a new campus AI system, giving students and staff access to a range of officially licensed mainstream AI tools. The university explicitly encourages students to make reasonable use of AI in their learning, and some business and engineering courses even require students to use AI for assignments. AI is banned only in closed-book in-class exams; it may be used legitimately for homework and research projects. The core principle is "use the tool well, no ghostwriting," with the emphasis on developing students' ability to work with AI.

Massachusetts Institute of Technology

In August 2026 MIT released a new framework for teaching with AI. Rather than banning AI, it classifies learning situations in detail, into four categories: AI prohibited, allowed with disclosure, allowed without disclosure, and AI required. At the same time it addresses AI cheating by reforming exam formats and adding hands-on projects, and in computer science and engineering in particular it integrates AI into everyday teaching as an essential learning tool.

University of Michigan

Michigan uses the most common regulatory model in the United States and does not ban AI. Students must complete a standardized AI-use statement for all core assignments, clearly stating whether AI took part in ideation, drafting, revision and so on. Transparency is the core principle throughout: tools are not restricted, only academic integrity is enforced, so that students cannot hide their use of AI.

Purdue University

Purdue updated its formal AI policy in April 2026 and explicitly encourages students to use generative AI responsibly. The university provides approved campus AI tools and prohibits students from using unauthorized personal AI tools to handle university research or private data. At the course level, instructors' rules still apply, and all AI-assisted content must be labeled.

My view

Overall, apart from Chicago and Stanford, which stand out, the other schools take a fairly neutral position: AI is allowed, but you have to disclose where you used it. I think this is a good approach. On one hand, teachers can use the AI trail to check whether a student has really improved their thinking by completing the task, and can see clearly which parts the student did on their own, which helps them guide students in using learning tools properly. On the other hand, students face few restrictions: as long as they are willing to label their AI use, they can do a task entirely their own way, and as long as they stay honest, many rule-based constraints fall away. I also very much agree with MIT's rule dividing AI use in assignments into four categories; it gives students a very clear rule for how to use AI properly so that their abilities get a full workout. Purdue's new policy of encouraging AI use while providing the AI itself is also very reasonable; supplying students with approved AI to protect data security is a safe approach.

Chicago and Stanford have the most distinct positions: one fully encourages students to use AI, the other bans it outright. My personal prediction is that both schools' rules will gradually change and converge toward the other schools' policies, because either over-encouraging AI or banning it too strictly brings its own problems.
